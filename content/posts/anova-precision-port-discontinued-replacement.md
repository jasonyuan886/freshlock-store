# Anova Precision Port Discontinued? Here's the Best Replacement in 2026

If you landed on this page, you were probably looking for the **Anova Precision Port** — Anova's cordless handheld vacuum sealer, model ANHV01-US00, popular with sous vide cooks — and found it out of stock or missing from Anova's store.

You're not imagining things. The Precision Port has been quietly discontinued, and as of 2026 its official product page no longer exists. If you own one, it still works (bags and valves are standardized), but if you're shopping new, you need a replacement. This guide covers what made the Precision Port good, what has changed in the handheld sealer market since, and which replacement actually matches or beats it.

---

## What was the Anova Precision Port?

The Precision Port was a compact, rechargeable handheld vacuum pump designed to pair with Anova's valve bags and containers for sous vide cooking and freezer storage. It earned a "Best Value" nod from Good Housekeeping in earlier testing, and it appealed to buyers who already owned an Anova circulator.

Its main strengths were a recognizable brand in the sous vide space, simple one-button operation, and an ecosystem of matching bags. Its limitations, per owner feedback, included modest suction compared with countertop machines, micro-USB era charging, and no liquid protection between the bag valve and the motor.

---

## What to look for in a replacement

The handheld sealer category has moved on. When replacing a Precision Port in 2026, compare these five specs — they're the ones that actually determine whether you'll keep using the device or leave it in a drawer:

1. **Published suction pressure (kPa).** This is the number that matters. Aim for -60 kPa or better; many brands don't publish the figure at all.
2. **Charging standard.** USB-C means you can charge anywhere and never hunt for a proprietary cable.
3. **Liquid protection.** A detachable drip tray between the valve and the motor is the difference between safely sealing marinated meats and ruining the pump. Most handheld sealers — including the Precision Port — have no barrier at all.
4. **Bag compatibility.** Valve bags are the ongoing cost. A pump that works with *any* brand of embossed valve bags saves money forever; a brand-locked pump forces you to buy refills from one source.
5. **Weight and noise.** Under ~250 g and under ~60 dB means you'll actually use it for weeknight meal prep.

### The two questions every handheld buyer asks

**"Can I seal soups and marinades without destroying the motor?"**

This is the single most common point of confusion about handheld sealers. Traditional pump-style sealers — the Precision Port included — pull air straight through the valve, so a loose marinade or soup liquid can travel up the nozzle and into the motor, which is usually fatal for the pump.

FreshLock Pro solves this with a **detachable transparent drip cup** that sits between the nozzle and the motor. Liquids pool in the cup instead of reaching the pump; the cup rinses clean under the tap. For very wet foods you still freeze portions partially or hold the bag upright, but the physical barrier means an accidental splash doesn't kill the device. No heat bar, no flood risk to the motor.

**"Am I locked into one brand of bags?"**

With the Precision Port ecosystem, you were steered toward Anova-branded valve bags. FreshLock works with **most embossed valve bags** — any 90μm+ PA+PE bag with a standard one-way air valve, from any manufacturer. The pump doesn't know the difference. Since bags are the recurring cost of vacuum sealing (the device is a one-time purchase), open compatibility usually pays for itself within a few months of weekly meal prep.
