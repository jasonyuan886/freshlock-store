# How to Vacuum Seal Homemade Pasta and Fresh Dough for Freezer Storage

Making fresh pasta or dough from scratch is deeply satisfying — but also time-consuming. The smart move is to make a large batch when you have time, vacuum seal portions for the freezer, and enjoy fresh pasta or pizza on busy weeknights with zero prep.

Vacuum sealing is superior to standard freezer bags for dough and pasta because it prevents freezer burn, stops ice crystal formation (which ruins texture), and keeps flavors pristine for months. Here is how to do it right.

---

## Vacuum Sealing Fresh Homemade Pasta

### Why Vacuum Sealing Beats Standard Storage

Fresh pasta in standard freezer bags develops ice crystals within 2–3 weeks, leading to clumping and mushy texture when cooked. Vacuum-sealed pasta stays separate, dry, and perfect for 3–4 months.

### Step-by-Step:

1. **Make your pasta.** Use a classic egg dough (00 flour + eggs) or semolina-water dough for extruded shapes.

2. **Cut to desired shape.** Fettuccine, tagliatelle, pappardelle, or cut into sheets for lasagna.

3. **Dry slightly.** Hang fresh pasta on a drying rack or draped over a broom handle for 15–30 minutes. The surface should feel dry to the touch but still flexible. This step is essential — wet pasta will clump and crush under vacuum pressure.

4. **Nest or coil.** For long pasta, form loose nests (twist a portion around your hand). For short pasta (penne, farfalle), leave loose.

5. **Flash freeze.** Place pasta nests on a parchment-lined tray. Freeze for 1–2 hours until solid. This prevents the vacuum sealer from crushing delicate shapes.

6. **Vacuum seal in portions.** Place frozen pasta nests in vacuum bags, seal with your [FreshLock vacuum sealer](/products/freshlock-starter-kit), and label.

**Shelf life:** 3–4 months frozen (vs. 2–3 weeks in standard bags).

### Cooking Vacuum-Sealed Frozen Pasta:

- **No need to thaw.** Drop frozen pasta directly into boiling, salted water.
- Cook for 2–4 minutes (fresh pasta cooks fast, even from frozen).
- Taste test — frozen fresh pasta is indistinguishable from freshly made.

## Vacuum Sealing Pizza Dough

### The Make-Ahead Advantage

Pizza dough benefits enormously from cold fermentation — a slow rise in the refrigerator develops complex flavor over 24–72 hours. Vacuum sealing extends this concept to weeks.

### Step-by-Step:

1. **Make dough.** Standard recipe: 500g flour, 325g water, 10g salt, 3g instant yeast, 15g olive oil. Knead for 10 minutes.

2. **First rise.** Let dough rise at room temperature for 1–2 hours until doubled.

3. **Portion.** Divide into individual pizza-sized balls (250–280g each for a 10–12 inch pizza).

4. **Pre-freeze.** Place dough balls on a parchment-lined tray, cover loosely, and freeze for 2–3 hours until firm but not rock-hard.

5. **Vacuum seal.** Place firm dough balls in vacuum bags, seal gently (use the gentle setting if available to avoid crushing).

6. **Freeze or refrigerate.**
   - **Refrigerator (vacuum sealed):** 5–7 days — cold fermentation continues, developing deeper flavor
   - **Freezer (vacuum sealed):** 2–3 months

### Using Vacuum-Sealed Pizza Dough:

- **From fridge:** Remove 1 hour before use, let come to room temperature, stretch and bake.
- **From freezer:** Thaw overnight in refrigerator, then proceed as above.

## Vacuum Sealing Cookie Dough

Cookie dough is one of the best make-ahead foods. Vacuum-sealed cookie dough lets you bake fresh cookies on demand — perfect for unexpected guests or late-night cravings.

### For Slice-and-Bake Logs:

1. **Form dough into logs** (5–6 cm diameter) on parchment paper.
2. **Roll tightly** in parchment, twist ends.
3. **Flash freeze** for 2 hours until firm.
4. **Vacuum seal** the parchment-wrapped logs in vacuum bags.
5. **Freeze** for up to 3 months.
6. **To bake:** Unwrap, slice 1cm thick rounds, place on baking sheet, bake at 175°C (350°F) for 10–12 minutes.

### For Pre-Scooped Cookies:

1. **Scoop dough** into balls using a cookie scoop.
2. **Flash freeze** on a tray for 1–2 hours.
3. **Vacuum seal** frozen dough balls in bags.
4. **Freeze** for up to 3 months.
5. **To bake:** Place frozen balls on baking sheet, add 1–2 minutes to normal bake time.

**Shelf life comparison:**

| Storage Method | Refrigerator | Freezer |
|---|---|---|
| Standard container | 3–5 days | 1–2 months |
| Standard freezer bag | 5–7 days | 2–3 months |
| Vacuum sealed | 1–2 weeks | 3–4 months |

## Vacuum Sealing Other Types of Dough

### Bread Dough

- **After first rise:** Punch down, portion, pre-freeze, vacuum seal. Freeze for up to 1 month.
- **After shaping:** Shape into loaves, freeze shaped, vacuum seal. Thaw overnight, second rise, bake.
- **Shelf life:** 1 month frozen. Longer than a month, yeast activity declines and rise quality drops.

### Pie and Tart Dough

- Form into disks, wrap in parchment, pre-freeze, vacuum seal.
- Freeze for up to 3 months.
- Thaw overnight in refrigerator before rolling.
- See our [flour and baking ingredients guide](/blog/2026-08-10-vacuum-sealing-flour-and-baking-ingredients-long-term-storage) for keeping baking supplies fresh.

### Dumpling and Wonton Wrappers

- Stack wrappers with parchment between each, pre-freeze, vacuum seal.
- Freeze for up to 2 months.
- Do not over-vacuum — too much pressure can crack thin wrappers. Use gentle setting.

## Critical Tips for Dough Vacuum Sealing

1. **Always pre-freeze before sealing.** Soft dough will be crushed and deformed by vacuum pressure. Pre-freezing to firm or solid state is non-negotiable. This is the same principle as [vacuum sealing delicate foods](/blog/2026-08-08-vacuum-sealing-delicate-foods-without-crushing).

2. **Use parchment barriers.** Wrap dough in parchment before bagging. This prevents the dough from sticking to the vacuum bag and makes removal clean and easy.

3. **Leave room for expansion.** Yeast dough continues to produce gas slowly even when frozen. Leave a little headspace in the bag rather than vacuum-sealing at maximum pressure.

4. **Label with baking instructions.** Write oven temperature and bake time directly on the bag so anyone can bake without looking up the recipe. See our [labeling system guide](/blog/2026-08-07-how-to-organize-and-label-vacuum-sealed-food).

5. **Portion for single meals.** One vacuum bag = one meal. This prevents thawing more dough than needed. Learn more in our [portion control guide](/blog/2026-08-07-vacuum-sealer-portion-control-and-food-budget-guide).
