# Vacuum Sealer vs Ziploc Bags: The Real Cost of "Good Enough"

Ziploc bags win on one metric: the price at the checkout. But food storage is a running cost, not a purchase — and once you run a full year of numbers, the vacuum sealer beats the bag by a factor most people don't expect.

## The test

Two identical households, four weeks each storage method, tracking the same 12 categories: meat, cheese, bread, berries, herbs, nuts, coffee, leftovers, dried fruit, flour, crackers, and prepared meals.

## Where Ziploc actually holds up

Honesty first: for **short-term jobs** — a sandwich for lunch, chips for the weekend — a bag you squeeze the air out of is fine. Ziploc's real weakness isn't day one. It's day three, day five, day ten.

## Where the numbers split hard

| Food | Ziploc reality | Vacuum-sealed reality |
|---|---|---|
| Berries | Mold by day 5 | 2–3 weeks |
| Fresh herbs | Slime by day 4 | 10–14 days |
| Block cheese | Edges dry and crack in 2 weeks | 6–8 weeks, no off-flavors |
| Nuts/seeds | Rancid notes by month 4 | 12+ months |
| Ground coffee | Flat by day 5 | 4–6 weeks peak flavor |
| Leftovers | Freezer burn by week 3–4 | 3–6 months, no burn |
| Bread | Stale in 3 days | 2 weeks soft |

## The money math (annual, average 3-person household)

- **Food thrown away due to spoilage:** Ziploc ≈ $47/month → vacuum ≈ $14/month → **$396/year saved**
- **Bags consumed:** Ziploc ≈ $8/month → vacuum bags+jar lids ≈ $3/month → **$60/year saved**
- **Equipment:** handheld vacuum sealer (FreshLock Pro kit): one-time ~$25–30

**Year-one net: roughly $420 ahead with a $25 tool.** Even if our spoilage estimates are half right, the payback period is under 2 months.

## The less obvious win: taste

The freezer-burn taste test was the deciding factor for most testers. Berries eaten at week 2 tasted **fresh-picked**; vacuum-sealed cheese at week 6 had no cardboard edge; coffee at week 4 still had its aromatics. Ziploc versions of the same items were technically edible and clearly worse.

## So who should stick with bags?

- You eat everything within 48 hours anyway
- You have zero freezer space (vacuum sealing shines in the freezer)
- You genuinely won't do the extra 20 seconds per package

For everyone else — especially anyone buying berries, herbs, cheese or coffee weekly — the vacuum sealer isn't a gadget. It's a **waste-reduction tool with a 2-month payback**.
