# Vacuum Sealer Wattage and Power Draw Explained

Power draw isn't something most people think about for a kitchen gadget — until they're running one off an RV battery, a portable power station, or a circuit that's already carrying a microwave and a kettle. Here's how vacuum sealer power requirements actually break down.

## Countertop Sealers: Plugged-In Draw

Most countertop vacuum sealers draw somewhere in the **100-200 watt** range while actively sealing (the heat-sealing strip is the main power draw, not the vacuum pump itself). That's comparable to a small microwave running briefly, but on a dedicated wall outlet — fine for a standard kitchen, more of a constraint for an RV or off-grid setup on a limited circuit.

## Handheld USB-C Sealers: A Different Model Entirely

A USB-C rechargeable handheld sealer doesn't draw continuous wall power while sealing — it runs off an internal battery that's charged separately, similarly to a phone. The actual **charging draw is typically under 10 watts** (standard USB-C charging speed), and that charge then powers 80-100 individual seal cycles before needing to be topped up again.

## Why This Matters Beyond the Electric Bill

| Scenario | Why Power Draw Matters |
|---|---|
| RV / camper use | Limited to inverter or generator capacity — a 150W countertop sealer can strain a small inverter, while a pre-charged handheld draws nothing at the point of use |
| Off-grid / power outage | A charged handheld sealer works with zero live power draw — useful for preserving food during an outage when the fridge/freezer contents are at risk |
| Shared kitchen circuits | Running a countertop sealer alongside other high-draw appliances (microwave, kettle, toaster) risks tripping a breaker on an older circuit; a handheld draws nothing at time of use |
| Portable power stations | A 10W USB-C charge is trivial for any portable power bank or solar setup; a 150-200W countertop unit is a meaningful load |

## The Practical Takeaway

If you're comparing power specs, the more useful number for a handheld isn't wattage at all — it's **seals per charge**. A USB-C sealer that gets 80-100 seals on a 2.5-hour charge effectively decouples "using it" from "drawing power," which is the real advantage in any power-constrained setting: camping, RV life, boats, or simply avoiding tripping a breaker during a busy cooking session.
