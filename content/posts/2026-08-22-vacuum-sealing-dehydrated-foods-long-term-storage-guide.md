# Vacuum Sealing Dehydrated Foods: The Ultimate Long-Term Storage Guide

If you own a food dehydrator, you already know the joy of turning fresh fruits, vegetables, and herbs into shelf-stable snacks. But here's the thing — **dehydrating is only half the battle**. How you store those dehydrated foods determines whether they last 3 months or 3 years.

That's where vacuum sealing comes in. Combining dehydration with vacuum sealing is the gold standard for long-term food storage, and it's a game-changer for anyone serious about reducing food waste, preparing for emergencies, or simply keeping a well-stocked pantry.

## Why Vacuum Seal Dehydrated Foods?

### The Science of Moisture and Spoilage

Even after dehydration, foods retain small amounts of moisture (typically 5-20%). This residual moisture, combined with oxygen exposure, leads to:

- **Oxidation** — vitamins A and C degrade rapidly in the presence of oxygen
- **Moisture reabsorption** — dehydrated foods are hygroscopic and pull moisture from humid air
- **Insect infestation** — pantry moths and weevils love dried fruits and grains
- **Flavor loss** — essential oils and volatile compounds evaporate over time

Vacuum sealing removes up to 99% of the air from the bag, dramatically slowing all these degradation processes.

## Step-by-Step: How to Vacuum Seal Dehydrated Foods

### What You'll Need

- A [handheld vacuum sealer](/posts/best-handheld-vacuum-sealer-2026) (like the FreshLock, which offers portable, cordless convenience)
- Vacuum sealer bags (smooth or textured, depending on your machine)
- Fully dehydrated foods (cooled to room temperature)
- Optional: oxygen absorbers for extra protection

### Step 1: Ensure Complete Dehydration

Before vacuum sealing, make sure your food is **fully dehydrated**. Test by:

- **Fruits:** Should be leathery or crisp with no sticky spots
- **Vegetables:** Should snap, not bend, when cooled
- **Jerky:** Should crack but not break when bent

Any remaining moisture will create a breeding ground for bacteria in an oxygen-free environment.

### Step 2: Cool Completely

Let dehydrated foods cool to room temperature (about 30-60 minutes). Warm foods create condensation inside the bag, which defeats the purpose.

### Step 3: Portion Before Sealing

Decide on portion sizes based on how you'll use the food later:

- **Snack packs:** Single-serving bags for on-the-go
- **Recipe packs:** Pre-measured combinations for soups, stews, or trail mix
- **Bulk storage:** Larger quantities for pantry rotation

### Step 4: Load the Bag and Seal

1. Place food in the vacuum sealer bag, leaving at least 3 inches of clean space at the open end
2. For powdery or small items (like dried herbs or fruit leather pieces), fold a paper towel at the top to prevent dust from being pulled into the vacuum port
3. Run the vacuum sealer along the open edge until the air is fully extracted
4. Check the seal — it should be smooth and even with no wrinkles or gaps

### Step 5: Label and Date

Always label each bag with:

- Contents
- Date of dehydration and sealing
- Approximate weight or quantity

## Pro Tips for Maximum Shelf Life

### Use Oxygen Absorbers

For storage beyond 1 year, add a 100cc oxygen absorber inside each bag before sealing. This removes any residual oxygen that the vacuum sealer might have missed.

**Oxygen Absorber Size Guide:**

| Bag Size | Oxygen Absorber |
|----------|----------------|
| Quart | 50-100cc |
| Gallon | 100-300cc |
| 5-gallon | 500-1000cc |

### Double-Bag Fragile Items

Crispy dehydrated items like kale chips or fruit chips can puncture bags. Double-bag them or use thicker vacuum sealer bags (4-6 mil).

### Check Seals Periodically

Every 3-6 months, check your sealed bags:

- If the bag has lost its vacuum (feels loose), re-seal it immediately
- Look for any signs of moisture, mold, or off-odors
- Rotate stock using the FIFO (First In, First Out) method

### Store in Cool, Dark Conditions

Even vacuum-sealed foods degrade faster in heat and light. Ideal storage:

- **Temperature:** 60°F or below (15°C)
- **Light:** Dark pantry, closet, or storage bin
- **Humidity:** Below 15% relative humidity

## Best Dehydrated Foods for Vacuum Sealing

### Top Picks

1. **Dried fruit leathers** — Perfect for kid snacks and camping trips
2. **Dehydrated soup mixes** — Pre-portioned, just add water
3. **Dried herb blends** — Custom seasonings that stay fresh for years
4. **Homemade jerky** — Far superior to store-bought, lasts 1-2 years sealed
5. **Dried mushroom mixes** — Porcini, shiitake, and more for cooking
6. **Powdered vegetable mixes** — For smoothies and baking
7. **Dehydrated pet treats** — Save money on pet food too

### Foods That Don't Seal Well

- **High-fat items** — Nuts with high oil content can go rancid even without oxygen; consider refrigeration
- **Very sharp items** — Bone-dried items with sharp edges may puncture bags; use protective inner wrapping

## Vacuum Sealing vs. Mylar Bags: Which Is Better?

| Factor | Vacuum Sealed Bags | Mylar Bags |
|--------|-------------------|------------|
| Cost per bag | $0.10-0.30 | $0.50-1.50 |
| Oxygen removal | Excellent (99%) | Good (with absorbers) |
| Puncture resistance | Moderate | Excellent |
| Light protection | Low (translucent) | Excellent (opaque) |
| Best for | 1-3 year storage | 5-25+ year storage |
| Ease of use | Very easy | Requires heat sealer |

**Bottom line:** For most home dehydrators storing food for 1-3 years, vacuum sealing is the most practical and cost-effective option. For ultra-long-term prepper storage (5+ years), Mylar bags with oxygen absorbers are worth the investment.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Sealing warm food** — Always cool to room temperature first
2. **Overfilling bags** — Leave space for a proper seal
3. **Ignoring powder dust** — Small particles in the seal area cause seal failure
4. **Not labeling** — You won't remember what's in each bag after 6 months
5. **Storing in heat** — Even vacuum-sealed food degrades faster above 70°F
6. **Reusing bags for wet foods** — Once used for dry dehydrated items, bags can be reused but only for similar dry foods
