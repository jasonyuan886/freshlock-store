# Sourdough Discard: 7 Ways to Use It (And How to Keep It Alive for Weeks)

If you bake sourdough, you know the guilt: every feeding produces a cup of discard that recipes tell you to throw away. You don't have to — and you don't have to bake with it today, either.

## First: the storage trick that changes everything

Sourdough discard keeps ~5 days in the fridge in a covered jar before it gets too acidic. **Vacuum-sealed in a mason jar, it holds for 3–4 weeks** — because the acetone-sour flavor that builds over time is largely oxidation. Pull out what you need, reseal the rest. No more "discard deadlines" dictating your baking schedule.

## 7 ways to actually use it

1. **Discard crackers** — mix discard with olive oil, salt and rosemary; roll thin; bake 20 min at 160°C/325°F. The easiest win on this list.
2. **Pancakes and waffles** — swap discard for half the flour and liquid in your usual recipe. Tangy, light, better.
3. **Banana bread** — 100g discard replaces an egg and adds depth. Your overripe bananas finally have a purpose.
4. **Flatbread/pita** — discard + flour + water + salt, hot pan, 90 seconds each side.
5. **Chocolate cake** — discard's acidity makes cocoa bloom. This one converts skeptics.
6. **Tortillas** — softer and more flavorful than store-bought, and they freeze beautifully (vacuum-seal them).
7. **Dog treats** — discard + peanut butter + oat flour, bake until crisp. Check with your vet first.

## The rule that keeps it safe

Discard is flour and water — low-acid when fresh. **Keep it refrigerated or frozen, always.** Room-temperature discard in a sealed jar is a botulism risk, vacuum-sealed or not. The vacuum seal buys you time in the fridge, not permission on the counter.

## Bottom line

Sourdough shouldn't generate waste guilt. Vacuum-seal the discard, bake when you feel like it, and let the crackers win you over first — they always do.
