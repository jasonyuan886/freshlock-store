# How to Vacuum Seal Eggs for Long-Term Storage

Eggs are one of the most versatile foods in your kitchen, but they also have a frustratingly short shelf life. Raw eggs in the shell last about 3–5 weeks in the refrigerator, and once cracked, you have maybe 2–3 days before they go bad.

If you buy eggs in bulk, raise backyard chickens, or simply want to reduce food waste, vacuum sealing is a game-changer. With the right technique, you can extend egg storage from weeks to months — and in some cases, up to a year.

Here is a complete guide to vacuum sealing eggs for long-term storage.

---

## Can You Vacuum Seal Whole Eggs in the Shell?

**No — and this is the most important rule.** Never vacuum seal whole eggs in their shells. The vacuum process removes air pressure, and an eggshell cannot withstand negative pressure. The shell will crack, and raw egg will leak into your vacuum bag, creating a mess and a food safety hazard.

Instead, you have two options for long-term egg storage:

1. **Crack and vacuum seal raw eggs** (refrigerator or freezer)
2. **Cook eggs first, then vacuum seal** (freezer)

---

## Method 1: Vacuum Sealing Raw Eggs

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Crack eggs into a bowl** — crack as many as you want to store. A standard vacuum bag holds about 6–8 cracked eggs comfortably.

2. **Whisk lightly** — whisk just enough to break the yolks and combine. Do not over-beat; you want to minimize air incorporation.

3. **Pre-freeze in portions** — pour the egg mixture into silicone molds or ice cube trays (1 cube ≈ 1 egg). Freeze until solid, about 4–6 hours.

4. **Vacuum seal the frozen portions** — pop the frozen egg cubes out and place them in a vacuum sealer bag. Seal on the "moist" or "gentle" setting if your sealer has one.

5. **Label and store** — write the date and number of eggs on the bag. Store in the freezer for up to 6 months.

### Why Pre-Freeze?

Raw eggs are liquid, and vacuum sealers struggle with liquids — the machine may suck the liquid into the motor. Pre-freezing solves this problem completely. For more tips on handling liquids, see our [guide to vacuum sealing soups and sauces](/blog/vacuum-sealing-liquids-soups-sauces-guide).

## Method 2: Vacuum Sealing Cooked Eggs

Cooked eggs vacuum seal beautifully and reheat well. Here are the best options:

### Hard-Boiled Eggs (Peeled)

- Peel hard-boiled eggs and pat dry
- Place in vacuum bag — do not stack more than 2 layers
- Vacuum seal and refrigerate for up to 1 week (do not freeze — texture becomes rubbery)
- Great for [meal prep](/blog/vacuum-sealer-meal-prep-guide) snacks and salad toppings

### Scrambled Eggs

- Cook scrambled eggs until just set (slightly underdone is better — they will finish cooking when reheated)
- Cool completely on a plate
- Portion into meal-sized servings
- Vacuum seal and freeze for up to 3 months
- Reheat in microwave for 60–90 seconds

### Quiche and Frittata Slices

- Bake your favorite quiche or frittata
- Cool completely, then cut into individual slices
- Wrap each slice in parchment paper, then vacuum seal
- Freeze for up to 3 months
- Reheat at 350°F for 15 minutes

## Food Safety Tips

- **Always label bags** with the date — frozen eggs all look the same
- **Thaw frozen eggs in the refrigerator overnight** — never at room temperature
- **Use thawed raw eggs within 1 day** of defrosting
- **Do not refreeze** thawed eggs — plan your portions accordingly
- If a vacuum bag loses its seal or shows ice crystals inside, the seal has failed — use the eggs immediately or discard
