# Vacuum Sealing 101: A Complete Beginner's Glossary of Terms

Vacuum sealing has its own vocabulary, and product listings often assume you already know it. Here's every term you're likely to encounter, explained simply.

## Core Terms

**kPa (Kilopascal)**
The unit measuring suction strength. Higher (more negative) numbers mean more air removed — -60 kPa removes roughly 95% of air, the strongest currently available in a handheld sealer.

**Embossed Bag**
A vacuum bag with a textured, channeled surface (usually a waffle or ribbed pattern) that lets air travel to the valve during suction. Smooth bags don't work with clip-on suction sealers.

**One-Way Valve**
The small plastic fitting on an embossed bag that air passes through during sealing, then closes to keep air from re-entering. This is what a handheld sealer clips onto.

**Chamber Sealer**
A type of vacuum sealer (usually commercial-grade) that vacuums the entire internal chamber, including the open bag inside it — better suited to liquids than external clip-on sealers, but bulkier and more expensive.

**External Sealer**
The category handheld and most countertop sealers fall into — suction is applied only through the bag's valve or open end, not the whole chamber.

**Drip Tray**
A small, usually detachable and transparent component that catches liquid pulled toward the sealer during use, protecting the motor from moisture damage.

**Freezer Burn**
Dehydration and oxidation damage to frozen food caused by air exposure — appears as grayish-white, dry, leathery patches. This is the primary problem vacuum sealing solves.

**Anaerobic vs Aerobic Bacteria**
Aerobic bacteria need oxygen to grow; anaerobic bacteria (including some dangerous strains like botulism) can grow without it. This is why vacuum-sealed food still requires proper refrigeration/freezing — removing oxygen doesn't make food shelf-stable on its own.

**Sous Vide**
A cooking method involving sealing food in an airtight bag and cooking it in a precisely temperature-controlled water bath. Vacuum-sealed bags are commonly used for sous vide because they eliminate air pockets that would otherwise cause uneven cooking.

**Water Displacement Method**
A manual, machine-free technique for removing air from a zip-top bag by slowly submerging it in water so pressure pushes air out before sealing the last opening. Less effective than a true vacuum sealer but requires no equipment.

**Shelf Life vs Freezer Life**
Shelf life refers to how long food stays good at room temperature (rarely applicable to vacuum-sealed raw food); freezer life is the more relevant metric for most vacuum sealing use cases, referring to how long frozen sealed food maintains quality.

**USB-C Rechargeable**
A charging standard using a reversible USB-C cable rather than disposable batteries or a proprietary charging dock — the same connector used by most modern phones and laptops.

**Seals Per Charge**
How many individual bag-sealing cycles a fully charged handheld sealer can complete before needing to recharge — a more practical spec than raw battery capacity (mAh) for comparing models.

## Putting It Together

Once these terms click, most vacuum sealer product listings and reviews become far easier to parse — the specs that matter (kPa, seals per charge, bag compatibility) stop being marketing jargon and start being a real basis for comparison.
