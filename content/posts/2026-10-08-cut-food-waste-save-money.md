# Food Prices Are Still High. Here's the $25 Kitchen Tool That Pays for Itself in 8 Weeks.

The USDA's latest numbers say what your receipt already knows: groceries aren't getting cheaper. What most households miss is that they're paying for food twice — once at the store, and again when it goes bad before they eat it.

## The second grocery bill

The average American family throws away roughly **$100–$125 of food per month**. Not scraps — edible food that spoiled, staled or freezer-burned before anyone got to it. Berries that molded, herbs that liquified, cheese that dried at the edges, leftovers that became science experiments.

That waste has one common cause: **oxygen and moisture getting to food faster than your schedule does**.

## The $25 fix

A handheld vacuum sealer (the FreshLock Pro kit runs about $25 with bags and the mason-jar hose) attacks the problem at the source:

- Berries: 5 days → 2–3 weeks
- Herbs: 4 days → 2 weeks
- Cheese: 3 weeks → 4–8 months
- Coffee: 1 week of peak flavor → 1–2 months
- Leftovers: 3–4 days → 1.5–2 weeks

## The five habits that make the math work

1. **Seal it the day you buy it**, not the day you notice it's dying. Vacuum sealing preserves; it doesn't resurrect.
2. **Portion for real life.** Single-serving bags get eaten; giant bags get ignored.
3. **Label with dates.** Half of "waste" is mystery containers nobody dares open.
4. **Freeze the second half.** Anything you won't eat in 4 days goes to the freezer, sealed.
5. **Keep a "use first" bin.** Sealed food lasts longer, but rotation still matters.

## The math, conservatively

Cut monthly waste from $110 to $40 (a realistic target for most families) and the sealer pays for itself in **under 2 months** — then returns about $800 a year, every year, on a $25 tool that fits in a drawer.

Grocery prices are out of your control. The second bill isn't.
