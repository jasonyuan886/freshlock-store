## Why Vacuum Sealing Works for Herbs

Herbs spoil primarily through two mechanisms: moisture loss (wilting) and oxidation (browning and blackening). When you store herbs in a standard bag or container, trapped oxygen continues to fuel enzymatic browning and aerobic bacterial growth. The herbs also lose moisture rapidly through transpiration, causing leaves to go limp within 2–3 days.

Vacuum sealing addresses both problems:

1. **Oxygen removal** slows oxidation and inhibits aerobic bacteria, preventing the blackening that makes herbs look and taste spoiled.
2. **Moisture retention** keeps leaves hydrated and crisp, because the sealed environment prevents moisture from escaping into the surrounding air.
3. **Reduced ethylene exposure** — vacuum-sealed bags limit ethylene gas accumulation, which would otherwise accelerate ripening and decay.

The result: herbs that would normally last 3–5 days in a plastic bag can last 10–14 days vacuum-sealed in the refrigerator, and several months in the freezer.

## Which Herbs Work Best for Vacuum Sealing

Not all herbs respond equally well to vacuum sealing. Here is a breakdown:

| Herb Type | Fridge Life (Bag) | Fridge Life (Vacuum Sealed) | Freezer Life (Vacuum Sealed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basil | 2–3 days | 7–10 days | 3–4 months |
| Cilantro | 3–4 days | 10–14 days | 3–4 months |
| Parsley | 4–5 days | 12–14 days | 4–5 months |
| Mint | 3–4 days | 10–12 days | 3–4 months |
| Rosemary | 7–10 days | 3–4 weeks | 6 months |
| Thyme | 7–10 days | 3–4 weeks | 6 months |
| Oregano | 5–7 days | 2–3 weeks | 5–6 months |

**Tender herbs** (basil, cilantro, parsley, mint) benefit the most from vacuum sealing because they spoil quickly and the oxygen removal dramatically extends their life. **Hardy herbs** (rosemary, thyme, oregano) already last a while but still gain significant shelf life extension.

## How to Vacuum Seal Fresh Herbs: Step by Step

### Step 1: Wash and Dry Thoroughly

Wash herbs under cold water to remove dirt and insects. Then dry them completely — this is the most critical step. Excess moisture inside a vacuum bag will cause herbs to turn mushy and promote bacterial growth despite the oxygen-free environment.

Use a salad spinner for tender herbs, then pat dry with paper towels. For hardy herbs, a gentle shake and paper towel pat is sufficient.

### Step 2: Choose Your Storage Format

**For refrigerator storage (1–2 weeks):** Place herbs in a vacuum bag with a slightly damp paper towel to maintain humidity without soaking them. Seal using your [handheld vacuum sealer](/products/freshlock-starter-kit).

**For freezer storage (3–6 months):** You have two options:

- **Whole leaves:** Spread herbs in a single layer on a tray, freeze for 30 minutes, then transfer to a vacuum bag and seal. This prevents them from clumping together.
- **Herb paste (best for basil and cilantro):** Blend herbs with a small amount of olive oil, portion into vacuum bags, and seal. The oil prevents freezer burn and gives you ready-to-use herb portions for cooking.

### Step 3: Seal and Label

Place the herbs in a vacuum bag, leaving enough space at the opening for a clean seal. Use your [FreshLock Pro](/products/freshlock-starter-kit) to remove air and seal. Label each bag with the herb type and date — this is especially important for freezer storage, where bags can easily get mixed up.

### Step 4: Store at the Right Temperature

- Refrigerator: 35–40°F (2–4°C) for tender herbs
- Freezer: 0°F (-18°C) for long-term storage
- Basil is sensitive to cold — store vacuum-sealed basil at room temperature if refrigerating causes blackening

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Sealing wet herbs.** Always dry herbs completely before vacuum sealing. Moisture is the enemy of vacuum storage.
2. **Crushing delicate herbs.** Vacuum pressure can crush tender leaves. Place them flat in the bag and avoid overpacking.
3. **Forgetting to label.** Frozen parsley and frozen cilantro look identical. Label everything.
4. **Reusing old bags for herbs.** Herbs absorb odors. Use fresh bags to prevent flavor transfer.
