# How to Vacuum Seal Tomatoes: Fresh, Sauce, and Sun-Dried Methods

Few foods capture summer like a ripe, garden-fresh tomato. But the same qualities that make tomatoes delicious — high water content, delicate skin, rapid ripening — also make them one of the most perishable items in your kitchen. A perfect tomato on Monday can be a moldy mess by Thursday.

Whether you grow your own, buy in bulk during peak season, or simply want to stop wasting tomatoes that go bad before you use them, vacuum sealing offers three distinct approaches. Each method suits a different purpose, and understanding when to use which is the key to year-round tomato enjoyment.

---

## Method 1: Vacuum Sealing Fresh Tomatoes (Frozen)

Fresh tomatoes cannot be vacuum sealed at room temperature — their high moisture content would be sucked into the sealer, and the anaerobic environment at room temperature risks bacterial growth. The solution is freezing.

### Step-by-Step:

1. **Wash and core.** Remove the stem end with a paring knife.
2. **Decide: peel or unpeeled.** Skins slip off easily after freezing, so you can freeze whole and peel later. For immediate use, blanch for 30 seconds, plunge in ice water, and slip off skins.
3. **Flash freeze.** Place whole or halved tomatoes on a parchment-lined tray. Freeze for 4 hours until solid.
4. **Portion and seal.** Place frozen tomatoes in vacuum bags, seal with your [FreshLock vacuum sealer](/products/freshlock-starter-kit), and return to freezer.
5. **Label** with date and variety.

**Shelf life:** Vacuum-sealed frozen tomatoes last **10–14 months** — nearly double the 6-month shelf life of standard freezer bag storage.

### Best Uses for Frozen Whole Tomatoes:
- Thaw and blend into sauces
- Chop into chili and stews (no need to thaw — grate frozen tomato directly into the pot)
- Roast from frozen for concentrated flavor

For more on how vacuum sealing extends food life, see our [food storage duration guide](/blog/how-long-does-vacuum-sealed-food-last).

## Method 2: Vacuum Sealing Homemade Tomato Sauce

This is where vacuum sealing shines brightest. Tomato sauce is a meal-prep powerhouse, and vacuum-sealed portions maintain fresh flavor for months.

### Making the Sauce:

1. **Core and quarter** 5 pounds of ripe tomatoes (Roma varieties work best for sauce).
2. **Simmer** in a large pot for 45–60 minutes until broken down and reduced.
3. **Blend** to desired consistency (chunky or smooth).
4. **Season** with salt, olive oil, and herbs. Add garlic, onion, or basil to taste.
5. **Cool completely** — this is critical. Warm sauce in a vacuum bag will expand and compromise the seal.

### Vacuum Sealing the Sauce:

Because tomato sauce is a liquid, you need to prevent it from being sucked into the sealer. Use one of these methods:

| Method | How It Works | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-freeze in bags | Pour sauce into vacuum bags, freeze until solid (4–6 hours), then vacuum seal the frozen block | Bulk storage (2+ cup portions) |
| Pre-freeze in containers | Pour into silicone molds, freeze, pop out blocks, vacuum seal | Portion control (1/2 cup portions) |
| Paper towel barrier | Fold a paper towel into the top of the bag above the sauce to absorb moisture | Small quantities, quick sealing |

For more on vacuum sealing liquids, see our [liquids and soups guide](/blog/2026-08-08-vacuum-sealing-liquids-soups-sauces-guide).

**Shelf life:** Vacuum-sealed tomato sauce lasts **8–12 months** in the freezer. Compare that to 3–4 months in standard containers.

### Pro Tip: Freeze in Flat Layers

Lay sealed sauce bags flat while freezing. Flat packages stack neatly, save freezer space, and thaw much faster than thick blocks. This technique works for all vacuum-sealed liquids — see our [freezer organization tips](/blog/2026-08-04-vacuum-sealer-freezer-organization-tips) for more stacking strategies.

## Method 3: Vacuum Sealing Sun-Dried Tomatoes

Sun-dried tomatoes are concentrated flavor bombs that are expensive to buy but cheap to make. Vacuum sealing preserves them perfectly for long-term pantry or freezer storage.

### Making Sun-Dried Tomatoes:

1. **Slice** Roma or plum tomatoes in half lengthwise.
2. **Remove seeds** with a spoon (optional — seeds add tanginess).
3. **Season** with salt, olive oil, and herbs (oregano, thyme).
4. **Dry** using one of:
   - Oven: 200°F (95°C) for 6–8 hours
   - Dehydrator: 135°F (57°C) for 8–12 hours
   - Sun: On screens in direct sunlight for 2–4 days (cover with cheesecloth)

5. **Test for doneness.** Tomatoes should be leathery and pliable, not brittle. No moisture should bead when pressed.

### Vacuum Sealing:

- **Dry-packed sun-dried tomatoes:** Place in vacuum bags, seal. Store at room temperature for 3–4 months or in the refrigerator for 6–8 months.
- **Oil-packed sun-dried tomatoes:** Submerge in olive oil in vacuum bags, seal. Refrigerate for 4–6 months. Do not store oil-packed tomatoes at room temperature — botulism risk.

**Shelf life comparison:**

| Storage Method | Room Temp | Refrigerator | Freezer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard container (dry) | 2–3 weeks | 1–2 months | 3–4 months |
| Vacuum sealed (dry) | 3–4 months | 6–8 months | 12+ months |
| Vacuum sealed (oil-packed) | Not safe | 4–6 months | 12+ months |

## Which Tomato Varieties Work Best?

Not all tomatoes are created equal for preservation:

| Variety | Best Method | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Roma / Plum | Sauce, sun-dried | Low water content, meaty, concentrated flavor |
| Cherry / Grape | Frozen whole | Small size, high sugar, great for roasting |
| Beefsteak | Frozen whole or sauce | Large, juicy, good for chunky sauces |
| Heirloom | Frozen whole | Delicate flavor preserved; texture changes so best cooked after thawing |

## Common Tomato Vacuum Sealing Mistakes

1. **Sealing warm sauce.** Always cool to room temperature or below before sealing to prevent bag expansion and seal failure.

2. **Overfilling bags.** Leave at least 3 inches of space at the top of the bag for a clean seal.

3. **Not flash-freezing whole tomatoes.** Soft tomatoes will crush under vacuum pressure if not pre-frozen. See our guide on [vacuum sealing delicate foods](/blog/2026-08-08-vacuum-sealing-delicate-foods-without-crushing) for more techniques.

4. **Storing oil-packed tomatoes at room temperature.** This is a botulism risk. Always refrigerate or freeze oil-packed tomato products.
