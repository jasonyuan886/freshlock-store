# Handheld Vacuum Sealer Won't Seal Liquids? The Freeze-First Method (and Why It Works)

If you've searched handheld sealer reviews, you've seen the #1 complaint in the category: **"it won't seal anything with moisture — liquid gets sucked straight into the unit."** It's so common that some review sites disqualify handhelds over it. The good news: it's a technique problem, not a broken machine, and one habit fixes it.

## Why liquids break the seal

Handheld sealers pull air through a one-way valve in the bag. When liquid reaches the valve, three things happen: the valve can't seat (wet gasket = leak), the pump ingests moisture (drips into the drip tray or worse), and the bag never reaches full vacuum. Heat-bar countertop machines avoid this by sealing above the liquid line — handhelds don't have that geometry.

## The fix: freeze for 20–30 minutes first

Place liquid-containing food (marinades, soups, stews) in the bag and freeze it until the surface is solid — usually 20–30 minutes for a thin layer. Then seal normally. The frozen surface lets the pump pull full vacuum without ingesting liquid, and the seal holds because the gasket stays dry.

This is the same method professional kitchens use, and it takes less time than cleaning a flooded drip tray.

## Two supporting techniques

**Pulse sealing.** Don't hold the button down continuously on borderline-wet foods. Short bursts let you monitor and stop before liquid reaches the valve.

**Keep the drip tray clean and seated.** If your unit has a removable drip tray (check your model), an overfilled or mis-seated tray is a common hidden cause of "it stopped working" complaints. Rinse and dry it weekly.

## What a handheld can't do

Even with perfect technique, a handheld valve-bag sealer is not the tool for large volumes of soup. For quart-plus quantities of liquid, freeze flat in a container first, then seal the solid block — or use a chamber sealer, which handles liquids natively. Knowing the boundary is what separates "this gadget is amazing" from "this gadget is useless."

## The one-paragraph version

Liquid food? Freeze 20–30 minutes first. Borderline moisture? Pulse it. Drip tray? Clean and seated. Soup for a crowd? Freeze flat, then seal — or get a chamber sealer. The machines aren't broken; the technique is.
