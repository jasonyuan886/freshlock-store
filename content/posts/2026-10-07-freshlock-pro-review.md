# FreshLock Pro Review: A Handheld Vacuum Sealer That Earns Its Drawer Space

Most kitchen gadgets fail one simple test: six weeks later, are you still using them? We put the **FreshLock Pro handheld vacuum sealer** through 60 days of real household use — jars, bags, sous-vide prep, even rapid marinating — to see if it sticks.

## What you get in the box

- Handheld sealer unit (USB-C rechargeable)
- 5 reusable vacuum bags with double-zip seals
- Mason jar attachment hose, both regular and wide-mouth
- Charging cable, spare seal gasket, quick-start card

Setup is genuinely zero: charge it, and you're sealing within two minutes of opening the box.

## What held up over 60 days

**Jar sealing is the killer feature.** The hose + lid method is foolproof once you learn the two rules (new lids only; band goes on after pumping). We sealed 60+ jars — berries, nuts, herbs, coffee — and had exactly **two failures**, both traceable to reused lids. Hit rate: 97%.

**Battery life is better than spec.** Rated "up to 200 seals per charge"; we logged 141 seals before the first low-battery warning, mixing jars and bags.

**Bags reseal well.** The double-zip tops held vacuum through a full month in the freezer with no detectable leak on re-check.

**Marinade hack works.** Chicken breasts, sealed with marinade under vacuum, absorbed flavor in **20 minutes** that normally takes 4 hours. This alone justifies keeping it within arm's reach.

## Where it falls short

- **It's not a chamber sealer.** Delicate items (soft berries, flaky crackers) can get crushed if you over-pump. Use the pulse approach — short bursts, check often.
- **Hose storage.** The jar hose is small and easy to misplace. We taped ours to the inside of a cabinet door.
- **Replacement gaskets** aren't sold everywhere — order a spare pack with your first purchase.

## Verdict

The FreshLock Pro earns its drawer space. It's the rare kitchen gadget where the marketing understates the jar-sealing use case — that's the real product. **Buy it if** you buy berries, herbs, cheese or coffee weekly, or waste food you meant to eat. **Skip it if** you genuinely never have leftovers.
