# Essential Vacuum Sealer Accessories: What You Actually Need

You have a [handheld vacuum sealer](/blog/best-handheld-vacuum-sealer-2026) — now what? While the sealer itself is the core tool, the right accessories can dramatically expand what you can do with it. The problem is that the market is flooded with accessories, and not all of them are worth your money.

This guide cuts through the noise. We will cover the accessories that genuinely add value, the ones you can skip, and how to build a cost-effective setup that covers all your food preservation needs.

---

## Must-Have Accessories

### 1. Reusable Vacuum Sealer Bags

If you buy only one accessory, make it this one. Reusable vacuum sealer bags are the single most cost-effective upgrade you can make.

- **Cost savings:** A single reusable bag can replace 50+ single-use bags, saving $30–60 per year
- **Eco-friendly:** Dramatically reduces plastic waste
- **Versatile:** Works for dry goods, wet foods, and everything in between
- **Easy to clean:** Dishwasher safe on the top rack

Our [reusable vacuum sealer bags guide](/blog/reusable-vacuum-sealer-bags-guide) covers everything you need to know about choosing and maintaining reusable bags.

**What to look for:** BPA-free material, thick multi-layer construction, and compatibility with your specific vacuum sealer model.

### 2. Vacuum Sealer Bags (Single-Use, for Long-Term Freezer Storage)

While reusable bags are great for everyday use, single-use bags have their place — particularly for long-term freezer storage where you do not want to wash and reuse bags that held raw meat.

- **Best for:** Raw meat, fish, and poultry for the freezer
- **Thickness:** Look for at least 3-mil thickness to prevent punctures
- **Embossed channel pattern:** Essential for handheld vacuum sealers to create proper suction

### 3. Jar Vacuum Sealer Attachment

A jar sealer attachment lets you vacuum seal Mason jars using your handheld vacuum sealer. This is one of the most underrated accessories for food preservation.

- **Best for:** Dry goods like rice, beans, nuts, coffee, and spices
- **Benefits:** Glass jars are completely odor-free, reusable indefinitely, and provide an airtight seal
- **Cost:** Typically $10–15 for a universal jar sealer

Learn more about vacuum sealing jars in our [mason jar vacuum sealing guide](/blog/how-to-vacuum-seal-mason-jars).

### 4. Bag Cutter or Sealing Strip Cutter

If you buy bag rolls (which are cheaper than pre-cut bags), you need a way to cut them cleanly. A dedicated bag cutter ensures straight, clean cuts that seal properly.

- **Cost:** $5–10
- **Alternative:** A sharp pair of scissors works, but a dedicated cutter is faster and more precise

## Useful but Optional Accessories

### 5. Vacuum Sealer Container Set

Vacuum-sealable containers are rigid plastic or glass containers with a valve that connects to your handheld vacuum sealer. They are excellent for:

- **Marinating** — The vacuum opens food pores, allowing marinade to penetrate deeper and faster
- **Storing delicate items** — Soft fruits, baked goods, and other items that would be crushed in bags
- **Meal prep** — Stackable containers make fridge organization easy

Check our [vacuum sealing vs containers guide](/blog/vacuum-sealing-vs-containers) to see how containers compare to bags.

### 6. Sous Vide Immersion Circulator

If you already vacuum seal food, you are halfway to sous vide cooking. Vacuum-sealed bags are required for sous vide, making an immersion circulator a natural companion to your vacuum sealer.

- **How it works:** Vacuum seal your food, then cook it in a precisely controlled water bath
- **Why pair them:** Perfectly cooked steaks, chicken, and fish every time
- **Cost:** $80–200 for a quality immersion circulator

Our [sous vide vacuum sealing guide](/blog/vacuum-sealer-sous-vide-guide) covers this combination in detail.

### 7. Label Maker or Freezer Tape

Not glamorous, but essential for organization. Labeling your vacuum-sealed packages with contents and dates prevents mystery meals and reduces food waste.

- **Options:** Masking tape + marker, dedicated freezer labels, or a compact label maker
- **Cost:** $3–15

## Accessories You Can Skip

### Bottle Stopper
While bottle stoppers that vacuum seal wine bottles sound useful, most handheld vacuum sealers do not create enough suction to meaningfully preserve wine. You are better off with a dedicated wine preservation system.

### Bag Storage Dispenser
These wall-mounted dispensers hold bag rolls and have a built-in cutter. Convenient, but not worth the $20–30 price tag when a drawer and scissors work fine.

### Universal Lids
Silicone lids that supposedly create a vacuum seal on any container. In practice, the seal is inconsistent and does not hold for more than a few hours.

## Maintenance Tips for Your Accessories

- **Reusable bags:** Wash with warm soapy water after each use; inspect for punctures; replace every 6–12 months
- **Jar sealer:** Wipe the silicone gasket clean after each use; store dry
- **Containers:** Check valves for food debris; clean with mild detergent only
- **Bag rolls:** Store in a cool, dry place; keep away from sharp objects

For general vacuum sealer maintenance, see our [cleaning guide](/blog/how-to-clean-handheld-vacuum-sealer) and [troubleshooting guide](/blog/vacuum-sealer-tips-and-troubleshooting).

## The Bottom Line

You do not need every accessory on the market. Start with reusable bags and a jar sealer — these two items alone will transform what you can do with your vacuum sealer. Add containers and a sous vide circulator later if your cooking style calls for them.

The beauty of a [portable vacuum sealer](/blog/portable-vacuum-sealer-benefits) is its simplicity. With just a handful of well-chosen accessories, you can preserve food, save money, and expand your cooking repertoire — all without cluttering your kitchen with gadgets you will never use.
