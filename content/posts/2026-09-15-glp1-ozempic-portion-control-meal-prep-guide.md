# Meal Prepping on GLP-1 Medication: Why Portion Size Matters More Than Ever

You made the chili you've made a hundred times. Same pot, same recipe. Except this time you eat maybe six spoonfuls, feel full in a way that's almost uncomfortable, and push the bowl away. Now there's a gallon of chili and one person who can't eat it.

That's the part nobody warns you about. It's not just that you're less hungry — it's that your entire cooking muscle memory was built for a different appetite. You cook for four, you eat for one, and the leftovers pile up until you're reheating the same container for the fourth time and it tastes like nothing.

## The Reheating Problem Nobody Talks About

Here's the thing about eating on Wegovy or Zepbound: you're not just eating less, you're eating slower and stopping earlier. That means the same food sits around longer. A normal portion of chicken and rice gets reheated three, four, five times before it's gone. By round three, the rice is mushy, the chicken is rubbery, and you're chewing something you genuinely don't want to swallow.

And when your appetite is already small, food you don't want to eat is food you won't eat. So it goes in the trash. You're not being wasteful — the food failed you.

The fix isn't cooking less. It's **packaging smaller, immediately, before the food ever hits the fridge as one big container.**

## Freeze in 4–6 oz Portions, Not "Servings"

Forget standard serving sizes. They're irrelevant to you now. What matters is what you'll actually finish in one sitting — and for most people on GLP-1 medication, that's somewhere between **4 and 6 ounces**.

So the night you cook, before you sit down to eat, do this:

1. **Portion everything into 4–6 oz containers while it's still hot.** Not after dinner. Now. Once it's in the fridge as one block, you'll never break it down.
2. **Let it cool to room temp (about 30–45 minutes), then vacuum seal each portion flat.** Flat packs freeze faster and thaw in a fraction of the time.
3. **Label with the date and contents.** Sharpie on the bag. You will not remember what's in there in three weeks.
4. **Freeze immediately.** Don't do the "I'll freeze it tomorrow" thing. Tomorrow doesn't come.

Now you've got ten small meals instead of one giant one. When you're hungry — and it might be at 2pm or 8pm or not at all — you pull out exactly one, thaw it, and eat it once. No reheating cycle. No texture death spiral.

## Why Vacuum Sealing Actually Matters Here

If you're eating mostly from the freezer instead of the fridge, freezer burn becomes your real enemy. Those 4 oz portions are small and thin — they'll dry out and get icy fast in a regular zip bag.

This is where vacuum sealing earns its keep. Pulling the air out at **-60 kPa** means no air pockets around your food, which means no ice crystals forming on the surface of your salmon or your shredded chicken. Small portions survive freezing dramatically better when they're sealed tight. You're not just saving space — you're protecting the only food you're going to eat that day.

It also means you can prep once and eat for three weeks without the food degrading. For anyone whose appetite is unpredictable, that's the whole game.

## Build a Freezer of Options, Not a Fridge of Obligations

The mental shift is this: stop thinking "what do I need to eat before it goes bad" and start thinking "what sounds good right now."

A stocked freezer of small, sealed portions gives you choices. Shredded pork, lentil soup, diced chicken, a single portion of lasagna. You eat what appeals to you, in the amount you can handle, and nothing rots in the back of the fridge because you couldn't face it a fourth time.

That's not a diet strategy. It's just how you stop wasting food and stop dreading meals.

## Bottom Line

Your appetite changed. Your portions should too. Cook like you always did, but break it down into 4–6 oz portions the same night, seal them flat, and freeze them before they ever become leftovers. You'll waste less, eat better, and stop reheating the same sad container five times a week.
