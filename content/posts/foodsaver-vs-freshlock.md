# FoodSaver vs FreshLock Pro: An Honest Side-by-Side for Mason Jar Households

Type "handheld vacuum sealer" into any search bar and two names dominate the results: FoodSaver, the decades-old category king, and a wave of newer challengers led by FreshLock Pro. If your main use case is mason jars — not just bags — the comparison tilts faster than the marketing suggests. Here's the full picture.

## The two-minute version

| | FoodSaver Cordless | FreshLock Pro |
|---|---|---|
| Unit price | ~$45–60 | ~$75 (bundle) |
| Jar sealing | Needs separate $15–25 adapter | Hose included, both mouth sizes |
| Bag system | Proprietary valve bags only | Any one-way valve bags |
| Suction (quart bag) | ~12 sec | ~9 sec |
| Battery | ~120 seals/charge | ~150 seals/charge |
| 2-year cost (bags+accessories) | ~$180 | ~$120 |

## Where FoodSaver wins

**Brand trust and retail presence.** You can buy FoodSaver bags in Walmart, Target, and half the grocery stores in America. If you like walking into a store and solving a problem today, that's real value.

**Ecosystem depth.** Handhelds, countertop units, bags, rolls, containers, marinade boxes. If you want one brand to cover every possible use case, FoodSaver has spent 30 years building exactly that.

## Where FreshLock Pro wins

**Jar sealing out of the box.** This is the single biggest practical difference. Mason jar vacuum sealing requires a hose attachment that presses over the lid's valve. FoodSaver sells this separately (and it's often out of stock). FreshLock includes both wide-mouth and regular-mouth hoses in the box. If jars are your primary use, this one line item decides the comparison.

**Bag economics.** FoodSaver's handheld only works with their proprietary valve bags — typically $0.50–0.70 per bag. FreshLock works with any generic one-way valve bag, which run $0.20–0.30. Seal five bags a week and the price gap pays for the unit within a year.

**Measured suction.** In our timing tests, the FreshLock Pro pulled a usable vacuum on a quart bag in about 9 seconds versus 12 for the FoodSaver Cordless. Both are fine; one is consistently faster.

## The honest drawbacks of each

**FoodSaver Cordless:** the proprietary-bag lock-in is the dealbreaker for heavy users. The adapter situation for jars is genuinely annoying. Build quality is good, app is fine, but the total cost of ownership climbs fast.

**FreshLock Pro:** newer brand with a shorter track record (launched 2026). Fewer retail touchpoints — mostly online. If you want a product you can touch in a store before buying, that's a real consideration. The 30-day return window and PayPal-backed checkout mitigate the risk, but it's a factor.

## Who should buy which

- **Buy FoodSaver if**: you want store availability, already own their bags, or plan to buy their countertop unit later.
- **Buy FreshLock Pro if**: mason jars are your main game, you seal more than a few bags a week, or you want the lowest two-year total cost.

## The bottom line

For bag-only, occasional use, either works and FoodSaver's retail availability is convenient. For jar-heavy kitchens — which is where vacuum sealing delivers its biggest wins — the FreshLock Pro's included jar kit and open bag ecosystem make it the better value on every timeline longer than a month.
