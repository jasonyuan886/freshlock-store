# Vacuum Sealer FAQ: 15 Quick Answers to Common Questions

**1. What does kPa mean on a vacuum sealer?**
It measures suction strength (negative pressure). Higher is stronger — -60 kPa removes roughly 95% of air from a bag, the strongest currently available in a handheld.

**2. Can I use any bag with a handheld vacuum sealer?**
Only bags with an embossed (textured) channel work — the texture is what lets air travel to the valve. Smooth bags like standard Ziploc bags won't work with a suction-based sealer.

**3. Is vacuum-sealed food safe from botulism?**
Removing oxygen can actually favor anaerobic bacteria like botulism in some conditions, which is why vacuum-sealed food still needs proper refrigeration or freezing — vacuum sealing is not a substitute for cold storage, it extends freshness within safe storage.

**4. How long does vacuum-sealed food last in the freezer?**
Typically 3-5x longer than non-sealed storage — many meats go from a 2-4 month freezer-burn window to 8-12+ months well-sealed.

**5. Can I vacuum seal liquids?**
Yes, with care — freeze very liquid items partially first, and use a sealer with a drip tray to protect the motor from liquid ingress.

**6. Do vacuum sealer bags work in the microwave?**
Most embossed vacuum bags are freezer- and boil-safe (check the specific bag's rating), but check for a microwave-safe label before microwaving directly in the bag — many are designed for sous vide/boiling reheating, not microwaves.

**7. How many times can I reuse a vacuum seal bag?**
Standard embossed valve bags can typically be reused dozens of times if washed and dried between uses, unlike single-use roll bags from some countertop sealers.

**8. Why isn't my vacuum sealer creating a strong seal?**
The most common causes are food debris in the sealing channel, an overfilled bag leaving too little flat surface to clip onto, or a worn valve seat that needs cleaning or replacement.

**9. Can a handheld vacuum sealer handle a whole turkey or large roast?**
Handheld sealers work best on single-meal-sized portions. For very large cuts, consider portioning into 2-3 sealed bags rather than one oversized bag, which strains the seal.

**10. Do I need batteries, or is it rechargeable?**
Modern handheld sealers like the FreshLock Pro use USB-C rechargeable batteries — no AA batteries to buy or replace.

**11. Is a handheld sealer as strong as a countertop model?**
Often comparable or stronger on suction alone — mid-range countertop models frequently sit at -40 to -55 kPa, while a strong handheld like the FreshLock Pro reaches -60 kPa.

**12. How do I clean a vacuum sealer?**
Wipe the sealing channel after each use, and detach and wash the drip tray regularly if you seal wet or juicy foods. Never submerge the motor housing in water.

**13. Can vacuum sealing help with meal prep for weight loss?**
Yes — pre-portioning meals to exact calorie/macro targets before sealing removes daily decision-making around portion sizes.

**14. What's the difference between vacuum sealing and canning?**
Vacuum sealing removes air but relies on refrigeration/freezing for safety; canning uses heat processing to make food shelf-stable at room temperature. They solve different problems and aren't interchangeable for long-term, unrefrigerated storage.

**15. Is it worth buying a vacuum sealer if I live alone?**
It depends on how much you currently waste — single-person households that buy in bulk or batch-cook still see strong ROI; those who cook fresh in small quantities daily see less benefit.
