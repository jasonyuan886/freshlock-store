# Before the First Winter Storm: The Vacuum-Seal Food Kit That Lasts 6 Months

Every October, the same story repeats across half the country: forecast shows a storm, stores sell out of bread and milk, and the family eats crackers for two days. A sealed emergency food kit ends that ritual — and it's a genuinely good use of a vacuum sealer.

## The 12-item kit (per person, 72 hours)

**No-cook proteins:** vacuum-sealed jerky, nuts, protein bars (jerky and nuts hold a year sealed)
**Carbs:** crackers in vacuum bags (6 months vs 2 weeks in the box), instant oatmeal packets
**Comfort + calories:** chocolate, peanut butter (jarred; no sealing needed), hard candies
**Drinks:** electrolyte powder, instant coffee (vacuum-sealed grounds keep 4–6 months)
**Extras:** dried fruit, emergency candles... fine, candles aren't food — but seal the matches with them

## Why vacuum sealing changes emergency food

Standard "prepper" buckets work, but they're built for years and taste like it. Vacuum-sealed kits are built for **this winter**, with food you'd actually eat:

- **Crackers:** 2 weeks in the box → 6 months sealed
- **Cheese:** 3 weeks → 4–8 months (real calories, real morale)
- **Dried fruit:** 6 months → 1–2 years
- **Coffee:** 1 month of drinkability → 6 months

Rotate once a year — eat the kit every October, rebuild the same week. It never expires on you unawares.

## The water caveat

Sealed food doesn't help without water. The rule: **one gallon per person per day, three days minimum** — and no, the vacuum sealer can't help you there. Store-bought gallons, rotated yearly.

## The one-hour build

Buy the list, seal in portions, write the build date on every bag, box it, and put it where you'll find it in the dark. One hour this weekend buys a whole winter of not-panicking.
