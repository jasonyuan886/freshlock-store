# The Thanksgiving Leftover Playbook: Vacuum-Seal It or Lose It

The average American household throws away about a pound of Thanksgiving leftovers per person. That's a whole second dinner — money, time and a very good turkey — in the trash. Here's the exact playbook, item by item.

## The timeline problem

| Leftover | Fridge, covered | Vacuum-sealed fridge | Vacuum-sealed freezer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roast turkey (sliced) | 3–4 days | 10–12 days | 3–4 months |
| Gravy | 1–2 days | 5–7 days | 2–3 months |
| Mashed potatoes | 3–4 days | 7–10 days | 1–2 months |
| Stuffing | 3–4 days | 7–10 days | 1 month |
| Pumpkin pie | 3–4 days | 7–10 days | 1–2 months (texture shifts) |
| Dinner rolls | 3–5 days | 2 weeks | 3 months |
| Cranberry sauce | 7–10 days | 3–4 weeks | 3 months |

## The moves that matter

**Slice the turkey before storing.** A whole carcass cools unevenly and dries at the surface; sliced meat in portioned bags reheats faster and wastes less. Add a spoonful of gravy to each bag — it's free insurance against dry reheats.

**Gravy is the first thing to die.** Two days, covered, is all it gets normally. Vacuum-seal in small portions (ice-cube trays first, then bag the cubes) and you've got gravy on demand through winter.

**Potatoes need flat packs.** Freeze mashed potatoes in thin, flat vacuum bags — they thaw in minutes under warm water and reheat without the gluey texture that big frozen blocks get.

**Stuffing: freeze, don't fridge-extend.** Its texture survives freezing surprisingly well, and vacuum-sealed it's a ready-made side for January nights.

**Rolls re-crisp.** Vacuum-sealed rolls at 2 weeks taste day-old, not week-old. Five minutes in a 150°C/300°F oven and no one can tell they weren't baked this morning.

## The weekend-after payoff

Vacuum-sealed turkey + gravy cubes + a bag of frozen stuffing = a full Thanksgiving dinner that reheats in 15 minutes, any weeknight through February. That's the real point of the playbook: one day of cooking, six weeks of returns.

## Safety note, once

Reheat to 74°C/165°F throughout. And if gravy sat at room temperature past two hours at the table, no storage method saves it — when in doubt, throw it out.
