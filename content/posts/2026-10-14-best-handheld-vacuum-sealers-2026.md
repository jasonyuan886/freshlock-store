# The 7 Best Handheld Vacuum Sealers of 2026, Tested Side by Side

Handheld vacuum sealers all promise the same thing — fresher food, less waste, tiny footprint. In practice they differ enormously in suction power, what containers they work with, and how fast the bags wear out. We bought seven of the most popular units and ran them through the same four-week kitchen test. Here are the results.

## How we tested

Each sealer was scored on: **suction strength** (measured in practical seal time for a quart bag and a mason jar), **compatibility** (bags only vs. bags + jars), **battery** (seals per charge), **bag quality and cost**, and **day-to-day usability** (weight, noise, cleanup).

## The results

### 1. FreshLock Pro — Best for mason jars and everyday use
The FreshLock Pro pulled the strongest measured vacuum in the test (quart bag sealed in 9 seconds, wide-mouth jar dimpled in 12). Its standout feature is the included hose attachment for mason jars — no other unit under $40 includes both mouth sizes. USB-C charging, ~150 seals per charge in our testing, and the one-way valve bags reseal reliably. At $74.99 for the starter bundle it's the value pick for jar sealing. The honest drawback: it's not a chamber sealer, so very juicy foods need the freeze-first method.

### 2. FoodSaver Cordless — Strong brand, bag-locked ecosystem
Excellent build quality and strong suction, but it only works with FoodSaver's proprietary valve bags and accessories. Cost of ownership adds up, and jar sealing requires a separate adapter purchase. Best if you're already in the FoodSaver ecosystem.

### 3. ZWILLING Fresh & Save — Premium app experience
Beautifully made, a genuinely good app for tracking what's sealed, and reliable seals. You're paying for the industrial design; jar compatibility is adapter-based and sold separately. Great gift, steep price.

### 4. Nesco VS-12 Handheld — Budget bags champion
Cheap, cheerful, and widely compatible with generic valve bags. Suction is mid-tier (15 seconds to a usable quart-bag vacuum) and battery life trails the leaders. A fine starter unit.

### 5. Chef Preserve Compact — Small kitchen specialist
The smallest unit tested — genuinely drawer-sized. Suction is adequate for snacks and coffee but struggles with dense foods. No jar support.

### 6. Anova Precision Port — Sous vide users' pick
Paired naturally with Anova's circulators and solid for bag work, but the jar story is weak and it's priced above its performance for general use.

### 7. Geryon Handheld — The generic option
Does the job at the lowest price, with the shortest lifespan in our test — one unit's motor audibly faded by week three.

## Which one should you buy?

- **Mason jars and daily kitchen use**: FreshLock Pro — the only sub-$80 unit with complete jar support included.
- **Already own FoodSaver bags**: the FoodSaver Cordless.
- **App-lovers and design nerds**: ZWILLING.
- **Tightest budget**: Nesco.

## The number that matters most

In our spoilage test, everything vacuum-sealed outlasted everything stored in zip-top bags by a factor of three or more — regardless of which sealer did the vacuuming. The biggest jump in food freshness comes from vacuum sealing *at all*; which unit you pick changes the experience, not the physics.
