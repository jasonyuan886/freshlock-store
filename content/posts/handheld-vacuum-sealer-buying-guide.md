### Short answer

Choose a handheld vacuum sealer by the bags, lids and containers you intend to use, not by a single suction number. Check the valve and nozzle fit, closure method, cleaning instructions, charging requirements and replacement accessories. If your job needs heat-sealed open-top bags, a valve-nozzle pump is not the same tool.

This guide is published by FreshLock, which sells handheld sealers. It is a practical buying checklist, not an independent lab ranking or a claim that FreshLock is best for every kitchen.

### 1. Match the sealing system to your food-storage routine

| Your intended package | Check before purchase |
|---|---|
| Reusable zipper vacuum bag | A compatible one-way valve, fully closing zipper and approved storage use |
| Mason jar or food container | A supported vacuum lid or explicitly compatible accessory, not just a familiar jar diameter |
| Open-top vacuum bag or roll | A device with the required heat-sealing system; do not assume a valve pump can close the opening |
| Wet or marinated food | Manufacturer's liquid-handling limits and cleaning procedure |

Consider how you use the food. A bag you reopen frequently creates a different routine from a large batch stored untouched. Choose suitable portion sizes and inspect the seal again after each opening.

### 2. Check compatibility before buying refills

For FreshLock Pro, the current published instructions describe embossed valve bags and lids with compatible one-way valves. The product page specifies the nozzle opening and required flat sealing surface. Verify the latest requirements on the [product page](https://www.freshlocksealer.com/products/freshlock-pro) and [FAQ](https://www.freshlocksealer.com/faq); third-party bag appearance alone does not prove a fit.

A standard flat Mason lid is not automatically compatible with this nozzle system. Nor does a conventional zipper bag become compatible merely because it is labelled for freezer use. For an unfamiliar lid, send support its specification or photo and request a compatibility check before ordering.

### 3. Treat suction as one specification, not a preservation promise

A pump-pressure specification does not by itself establish the oxygen remaining in your food package, the number of days food stays safe, or how long an unfamiliar bag will hold a seal. Package integrity, closure cleanliness, food handling and storage conditions also matter.

Look for clear, documented instructions instead of unsupported multipliers such as a universal five-times-longer claim. Do not compare different brands' pressure numbers unless their measurement conditions are comparable.

### 4. Understand liquid handling and maintenance

FreshLock describes a collection cup and drainage arrangement for handling collected moisture. These features still require correct setup, inspection and cleaning; they are not permission to draw unlimited liquid into the device or a guarantee that a motor cannot fail.

Keep the valve area clear, follow the manual's wet-food procedure and stop if liquid movement is not behaving as instructed. Do not wash the powered body unless the manual expressly permits it. Verify the cleaning method for each removable part before using it.

### 5. Compare the full ownership cost

Check current kit contents, refill prices, lid or container accessories, delivery charges, returns and warranty coverage on the seller's current pages. Do not choose from an old comparison table whose prices or delivery estimates no longer match checkout.

Reusable does not mean indefinitely reusable. Inspect the closure, valve and material, and follow the package manufacturer's cleaning, food-contact and reuse restrictions. Never infer microwave, dishwasher or sous-vide suitability from the word vacuum alone.
