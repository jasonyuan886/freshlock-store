# Meal Prep That Doesn't Taste Like Meal Prep: The Vacuum-Seal Method

Standard meal prep has a fatal flaw: everything you cook on Sunday tastes like leftovers by Wednesday. The cause is oxygen, and the fix is a $25 vacuum sealer plus a different packing method.

## Why Wednesday is where meal prep dies

Cooked food in regular containers keeps 3–4 days. Prep Sunday, eat Monday–Wednesday fine, and Thursday–Friday you're forcing down food that's technically safe but tastes tired. That's when people quit meal prep — not from laziness, from flavor fatigue.

Vacuum-sealed, cooked food holds **8–12 days refrigerated**. The whole week becomes Sunday-fresh.

## The 5-day vacuum-prep plan

**Sunday (90 minutes):**
- Cook 2 proteins (chicken thighs + a beef or lentil option), 1 grain, 2 vegetable trays
- Cool everything completely — warm food in a sealed bag is the one real safety mistake
- Portion into single meals, vacuum seal flat

**The packing order that matters:**
- Proteins with a spoonful of sauce or marinade — reheats juicy, not dry
- Grains plain — they reheat better separately
- Roasted vegetables in their own bags — they keep texture instead of steaming

**Daily:** cut one corner of the bag, microwave 90 seconds, or drop the whole bag in simmering water for 5 (sous-vide style reheating is the pro move that changes everything).

## The numbers

- Prep time: ~90 minutes Sunday for 10 meals
- Cost per meal: typically $3–5 vs $12+ takeout
- Weekly savings: $70–90 for one person
- Food actually eaten, not trashed: near 100% vs the ~30% waste of container-based prep

## What NOT to vacuum-prep

- Crispy things (they soften — pack those day-of)
- Leafy salads (vacuum pressure bruises them; use jars for salad components)
- Anything still warm. Ever.

## The gateway habit

Start with just proteins. One Sunday, seal five portions of chicken, and compare Thursday's vacuum-sealed piece against a container piece. The difference ends the debate — and your takeout habit follows within a month.
