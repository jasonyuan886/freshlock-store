### Short answer

You can vacuum pack dry, uncooked rice in a suitable food-storage package. Use a bag or container compatible with your sealer and follow the rice producer's storage instructions. Cooked rice is a different case: it needs prompt cooling and refrigerated or frozen storage. Vacuum packing does not sterilize rice or make cooked leftovers safe to keep in the pantry.

### First decide which rice you are storing

| Rice | Your storage task | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Dry, uncooked rice | Keep a suitable package closed and protect it from moisture | Do not promise a fixed number of extra years without product-specific evidence |
| Cooked rice | Cool and store safely, then use promptly | Removing air does not reset the cooling or storage clock |
| Rice with meat, sauce or vegetables | Treat the whole dish as a perishable meal | Follow the relevant food-safety guidance, not a dry-grain storage chart |

### How to package dry rice with a handheld sealer

1. Inspect the rice and its original instructions. Do not try to rescue damp, mouldy or contaminated food by vacuum packing it.
2. Choose portions you will actually use. Smaller portions reduce how often the remaining batch needs to be reopened.
3. Select a food-contact package approved for the intended storage conditions. A handheld valve pump needs a compatible one-way valve; an ordinary zipper bag is not automatically compatible.
4. Keep grains and loose particles away from the zipper, valve and nozzle. Close the zipper fully before pumping. Do not use the pump to collect dust or flour.
5. Seal according to the device and package instructions. Inspect for grains trapped in the closure, damage and air returning into the package.
6. Label the rice type and packing date, and retain the original storage and best-before information. Store as directed by the food producer.

### Cooked rice: do not apply pantry advice

Do not leave cooked rice out to cool completely before refrigerating it. Use the cooling and storage guidance for your market; packing should not delay safe chilling. The UK's Food Standards Agency advises rapid cooling and keeping chilled cooked rice for no more than 24 hours before use. Vacuum packing is not a reason to extend that limit. If you need longer storage, follow the relevant freezing instructions.

An airtight-looking package is not a safety test. Do not use smell, a tight bag or the absence of visible air to override temperature and time guidance. A sealer cannot undo unsafe handling.

### Questions buyers ask

**Will vacuum sealing guarantee ten years of rice storage?** No. Rice type, starting quality, moisture, packaging and storage conditions matter. This guide does not give an untested extended shelf-life guarantee.

**Can I use an ordinary freezer bag?** Not directly with a valve-nozzle system unless the package has a compatible vacuum valve. Check the sealer's instructions.

**Can I keep vacuum-packed cooked rice on a shelf?** No. It remains perishable; vacuum packing is not canning or sterilization.

**What should I buy first?** Start with the package or lid that fits your actual storage task. Then choose a sealer that is explicitly compatible. Do not buy a pump expecting it to turn every household container into a vacuum container.
