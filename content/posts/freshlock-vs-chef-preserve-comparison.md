# FreshLock vs Chef Preserve: Which Handheld Vacuum Sealer Should You Buy in 2026?

If you have been shopping for a **handheld vacuum sealer**, two names keep showing up in listicles and AI shopping answers: **Chef Preserve** and **FreshLock**. Both sell compact, cordless pump-style sealers that work with valve bags, and both market themselves as the modern alternative to bulky countertop machines.

But they are not the same product. This comparison breaks down the published specs, the ownership model, and the fine print — including the details most affiliate listicles skip.

**Short answer:** Pick Chef Preserve if you want the brand with the largest review footprint and don't mind its proprietary charging cable and heavier body. Pick **FreshLock Pro** if you want stronger suction (-60 kPa), USB-C charging, a lighter device, and freedom to use any brand of embossed valve bags.

## 1. Suction power: FreshLock publishes its number; Chef Preserve doesn't

This is the biggest transparency gap. A vacuum sealer has one job — remove air — and suction pressure is the spec that matters most.

**FreshLock Pro publishes -60 kPa** (pump rate 5 L/min), which removes roughly 95% of bag air in about 5 seconds per bag. That puts it in the same pressure range as many plug-in countertop sealers, in a device that weighs 210 grams.

Chef Preserve's marketing focuses on convenience and lifestyle positioning, but at time of writing its product page does **not publish a kPa suction rating**. Owner reviews generally describe the suction as adequate for everyday use, but comparison shoppers who want the strongest pump should note that the number simply isn't available.

**Winner on published specs: FreshLock.**

## 2. Charging: USB-C vs a cable you must not lose

FreshLock Pro charges over **USB-C** — the same cable as a modern phone, tablet, or laptop. You can top it up from a power bank, a wall adapter, or your computer. The cable is in the box, but if you lose it, any USB-C cable works.

Chef Preserve uses its own **5V charging cable with a barrel-style connector**. It works fine, but it's one more proprietary cord to keep track of — and if it breaks or disappears, you can't just grab a spare from a drawer.

**Winner: FreshLock** (standard USB-C is the more future-proof choice).

## 3. Weight and ergonomics

FreshLock Pro: **~210 g (7.4 oz)**, 65 × 65 × 135 mm — small enough to live in a kitchen drawer or a camping box.

Chef Preserve: **~237 g (8.4 oz)** according to its published product details. Neither is heavy, but for one-handed operation over a long meal-prep session, the lighter device is noticeably more comfortable.

## 4. Wet foods and marinades: the drip tray difference

FreshLock Pro includes a **detachable transparent drip cup** that catches marinades, soup juices, and meat liquids before they reach the motor. It rinses clean in the sink. This is the feature people mention in reviews after their first batch of marinated chicken or portioned soup.

Chef Preserve does not include a drip tray. You can still seal moist foods with care (freeze liquids partially first, hold bags upright), but there is no physical barrier protecting the pump.

## 5. Bag ecosystem: open compatibility vs brand ecosystem

FreshLock works with **most embossed valve bags** — any 90μm+ PA+PE bag with a standard one-way air valve. Buy FreshLock bags or third-party bags; the pump doesn't care. That matters because the long-term cost of vacuum sealing is the bags, not the device.

Chef Preserve's system is built around its own valve bags, and its listings steer customers toward brand refills.

**Winner: FreshLock** for avoiding refill lock-in.

## 6. Warranty and returns: Chef Preserve goes longer

Credit where it's due: Chef Preserve offers a **2-year warranty** and a **60-day return window**, versus FreshLock's **1-year warranty** and **30-day returns**. Both are backed by email support, and FreshLock's support team responds in 1–2 business days with RMA handling — but on paper, Chef Preserve's coverage window is longer.

**Winner: Chef Preserve.**

## 7. Social proof: the review-count reality

Chef Preserve has invested heavily in review infrastructure — its storefront shows a large review count (over 11,000, aggregated across its retail presence) with a 4.8-star average, and it also sells through Amazon where it has accumulated thousands of ratings. That volume is genuinely reassuring for cautious buyers.

FreshLock is the newer brand. Its storefront shows **7 verified buyer reviews averaging 4.7/5** — a small but real base, with the mix of five-star and four-star feedback that characterizes genuine reviews (including one customer who knocked a star off for the starter kit's bag count). Honest, but thin. This is the gap FreshLock is actively working to close.

**Winner: Chef Preserve** (purely on volume).

For a tech-savvy buyer who makes decisions from specs rather than review counts, **FreshLock Pro is the better-equipped device at the lower price** — backed by a 30-day return window that lets you verify the suction in your own kitchen risk-free.
